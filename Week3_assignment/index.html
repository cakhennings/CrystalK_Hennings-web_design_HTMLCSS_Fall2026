Tennis Is for Everyone

The Evolution of Tennis

Can you name a game that can be enjoyed by anyone, no matter how big or small or how old? If you thought tennis, you are right! The game, thought to have originated with medieval knights and monks in 12th century France, has evolved over the centuries. From playing with bare hands and gloves in its earliest form to playing with high technology racquets today, the game has changed significantly. What has not changed is how much fun one can have participating in the sport. For those unfamiliar with the sport, the United States Tennis Association (USTA) provides the following summary of tennis gameplay:

Each player or team has a maximum of one bounce after the ball has been hit by their opponent to return the ball over the net and within the boundaries of the court. In wheelchair tennis, players get a maximum of two bounces. When a player then fails to return the ball into the correct court, the opponent wins a point. The aim of tennis is to win enough points to win a game, enough games to win a set, and enough sets to win a match.

Benefits of Tennis

Tennis is a sport that can be enjoyed by most people, no matter their age or size. While it may be played in private clubs, it is often enjoyed on public tennis courts, accessible to most. While tennis is fun to play and a great source of physical activity, it also has benefits beyond the physical. Tennis can bring together strangers and promote social activity as much as physical. During a time when people long for connection, tennis can be a great way to meet and engage with others and even make lifelong friends.

Key Takeaways

In summary, tennis is a sport that I recommend for all because:

  1. Tennis is fun
  2. Tennis promotes social interaction
  3. Tennis is great exercise

Glossary of Terms

Advantage Set
In an advantage set, a player or team needs to win six games, by two, to win the set. This means that there is no tiebreak game played at 6-6. The set continues until one player/team wins by two games.
Deuce
When both players have won three points each and the score is 40-40.
Tiebreak Set
In a tiebreak set, a player or team needs to win six games to win a set. If the score gets to 5-5 (5-all), one player must win the next two games to win the set. If the score reaches 6-6 (6-all) in the set, a tiebreak game is played.
